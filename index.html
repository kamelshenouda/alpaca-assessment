I chose one of the recent achievements that is related to the wealth-tech industry.
Taking IPO subscriptions at Awaed from a stuck, never launched product to a live, end to end experience in —.

Context

  • Awaed is a Saudi WealthTech platform offering retail investing across Saudi/US markets.
  • IPOs are one of the biggest moments for Saudi retail investors, and a natural way to bring new investors in.
  • This was my first major challenge in WealthTech, in a market and a regulatory setup I had never worked in before.

Problem

  • The IPO product idea existed, but it had never been launched.
  • Running an IPO touches Tadawul, compliance, the CMA's rules, trading operations, the brokerage infrastructure, engineering, product design, and finally the user.
  • Each team held one piece of the story. No one held it end to end.
  • Tadawul's subscription file specs alone had dozens of fields, rejection codes, and several reconciliation reports.
  • There was no senior product ownership to bridge the gaps, identify the problem, define it into phases, and turn it into something shippable.

I owned it end to end, from understanding the problem to getting it live.

  3. 3Map one end-to-end journeyUsers and operations on the same page, from prospectus to listing.
  4. 4Define a shippable scopeTogether with engineering.

What I did
Went to Riyadh and sat with each team before writing a single requirement.
The alternative
Collect requirements remotely and stitch documents together.
Why I didn't
Each team only knew its own piece. The gaps sat between the teams, and they only show up when you walk the full process.
Go to the source. The real problem lives where the work happens.
What I did
Instead of waiting for a fully automated Tadawul integration, we launched with a file exchange every 2 hours, run by trading operations, who already had Tadawul access.
The trade-off
Some manual work for operations, in exchange for going live in weeks, not months.
What I refused to cut
Anything compliance or Tadawul required.
Scope is the lever. Compliance and correctness are not.
What I did
Mapped the operations journey with the same care as the user journey: who creates the IPO, who exports and uploads files, who pushes allocations, and what happens when Tadawul rejects a subscription.
Why
A beautiful app is worthless if the back office can't run the process reliably every 2 hours.
Operations is a user too. If the back office breaks, the experience breaks.
Simple statuses
Tadawul returns many result codes and reports. Users see a few clear statuses, each with a notification:
ReceivedAccepted or RejectedFully or Partially AllocatedCancelled
Money held, not taken
The amount is held at subscription. After allocation, we deduct only what was allocated and release the rest automatically.
Users should always know where their money is, without needing to understand how an exchange works.

Once the Saudi flow worked end to end, I took the same model to the US market. The journey was familiar; the infrastructure was not.

Omnibus accountEvery order goes through one Awaed account. The provider sees one investor, while we hold many individual subscriptions.
Limited allocation flexibilityWe receive one combined allocation and final price, and have to divide it among our users ourselves.
New market rulesPrice ranges instead of fixed prices, FINRA Rules 5130 & 5131 attestation, USD offerings for SAR-based users, and lock-up rules after allocation.
The real dilemmaA fully digital integration would take months. A manual flow could go live in weeks.

I split the problem in two, so speed and scale didn't have to compete.
